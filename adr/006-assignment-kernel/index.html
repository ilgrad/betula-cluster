    ADR 006 — one exact kernel for nearest-centre assignment, and the pruned plan only behind a hint¶

    Status: Accepted · records how predict finds a row's nearest centre or leaf in the release after 1.5.0. Through 1.5.0 the estimator's predict labelled a row by a serial scalar f64 loop over the centres. The pruned plan in src/assign.rs shipped in 1.0.0 with no caller; the rows head (leaf_graph="rows") is its first.

    Context¶

    The crate has two exact ways to find the nearest of k points to a row.

    1. The full scan, kernels::nearest_sq_euclidean. It takes the CPU dispatch once per scan and runs the packed AVX2 + FMA kernel from 16 coordinates, the serial loop below that. It keeps a candidate only on a strict <, in index order.
    2. The plan, AssignPlan. It sums each candidate's squared distance in stages, in descending order of between-centroid variance, and stops a candidate as soon as its partial sum is strictly greater than the best full distance so far. From d = 128 a stage is 64 coordinates (assign::default_block). A hint names a first candidate, whose distance becomes the starting threshold. The plan decides on the pair (distance, index), so a hint changes the work and never the answer.

    The bound in the plan is exact: a running sum of non-negative terms does not decrease under round-to-nearest, so a partial sum above the threshold proves the full sum is above it too.

    Two callers need a nearest point.

    • A centre rule labels a row by its nearest centre: kmeans, xmeans, kmedoids and spherical-kmeans, with or without a projection, and fuzzy-cmeans, whose memberships come from the same distances. It has no previous label to use as a hint.
    • The nearest-leaf rule of the rows head labels a row by its nearest of about 4 000 leaves. The tree descent gives every row a leaf, which is a hint.

    The scalar loop was the most expensive part of predict. At 256 centres it retired 32.5 G instructions on MNIST-20k (d = 784) and 14.3 G on the 17 901 rows of 20 newsgroups (MiniLM, d = 384), float32.

    The plan's own benchmark (src/assign.rs) has it losing to a scan below d ≈ 128 even with a hint, and winning 2–8× from d ≈ 784 with one. Without a hint it had not been measured on real data. It was measured here on a kmeans rule, float32 rows, max_leaves=2000, threshold=0.0, seed 0, one thread, retired instructions of one predict of the training rows: MNIST-20k, 20 newsgroups embedded by MiniLM (17 901 rows) and by MPNet (18 119), and 20 000 rows drawn from CIFAR-100 embedded by CLIP:

    set d k = 128 256 512 1024
    MNIST 784 2.22 → 1.63 G 4.18 → 2.51 G 8.10 → 4.03 G 15.95 → 6.79 G
    20 newsgroups, MiniLM 384 1.01 → 1.65 G 1.91 → 2.77 G 3.70 → 4.77 G 7.29 → 8.34 G
    20 newsgroups, MPNet 768 1.97 → 2.79 G 3.71 → 4.50 G — 14.17 → 12.58 G
    CIFAR-100, CLIP 1280 3.57 → 3.81 G 6.72 → 5.72 G — 25.63 → 13.31 G

    Each cell is full scan → plan. Every cell returned the same labels both ways. The plan wins on MNIST from k = 128 and on CLIP from k = 256. It loses on MPNet up to k = 256 and on MiniLM at every k measured. Why it loses there was not measured. Two causes are plausible: a flat variance spectrum, which leaves little of the distance in the first stages, and the dispatch. Each stage calls kernels::sq_euclidean through its run-time dispatch, which the full scan pays once per scan. Building the plan costs about 30 full scans, so a one-row call with a plan retired 6.9 M instructions against 0.23 M for the scan (MNIST, k = 256).

    Decision¶

    1. The distance is exact, with no rotation and no statistical bound. A prefix bound prunes without an error budget, and pruning cannot change a label: every comparison is between two sums accumulated the same way. SuperKMeans's ADSampling bound (arXiv 2603.20009) was rejected in 1.0.0 and stays rejected: its Haar rotation costs d² per row, which at the k of a CF summary is more than the assignment it speeds up.

    2. Every centre rule takes the full scan. For Centers and Fuzzy rules, dense and CSR rows, one row or many, predict widens each row to f64 and scans it with nearest_sq_euclidean, the kernel the one-shot function already labelled its rows with, in the tree's dtype. Fuzzy memberships use sq_euclidean over the same rows, so predict_proba(X).argmax(1) names the centre predict names. A centre rule builds no plan, at any width, any centre count or any row count.

    3. The plan runs only where a hint exists. The nearest-leaf rule has one in the descent's leaf. The graph pass and predict run the same hinted search in the tree's dtype, so a training row's label is its leaf's community by construction, and fit_predict reads each row's leaf off the graph pass. The plan is cached per tree dtype and built on first use.

    4. d ≥ 128 is a necessary condition for the plan, not a sufficient one. Below d ≈ 128 it loses even with a hint. From d = 128 the stage is 64 coordinates. Without a hint, d ≥ 128 and k ≥ 256 still lose on two embedding sets, so no fixed gate on d and k is adopted.

    5. Of two candidates at equal distance, the lower index wins, on every path. The scan keeps only a strictly smaller distance, in index order. The plan prunes only on a strictly greater partial sum, so a tied candidate survives, and it decides on (distance, index).

    6. The order of the sum is not preserved, and the labels are a measurement. From 16 coordinates the packed kernel sums in a different order from the serial loop, and the plan sums in its own stages. A distance moves by a few ulp, about 1e-13 relative in f64, so an argmin decided by less than that can flip. On MNIST-20k and MiniLM, over kmeans at their own k and at 300, xmeans, kmedoids and fuzzy-cmeans in both dtypes, the smallest relative gap between a row's two nearest centres was 2.5e-7, and no row sat on an exact tie.

    Consequences¶

    predict on a centre rule retires 3.5–8.1× fewer instructions, more with more centres. On the same rows: MNIST 1.49 → 0.41 G at 10 centres, 32.5 → 4.18 G at 256 and 129.2 → 15.9 G at 1 024; MiniLM 1.21 → 0.26 G at 20, 14.3 → 1.91 G at 256 and 56.9 → 7.29 G at 1 024. The label digests equal those of the build before it on both sets, both dtypes and every predict route. Fuzzy memberships move by at most 6.7e-16.

    A row is labelled alike alone and in a batch. One kernel answers every call size, so predict(X)[i] equals predict(X[i:i+1]) by construction. A plan gated on the row count would lose that at a near-tie.

    Nothing new is saved. The rule is the same list of centres, and models saved by 1.5.0 load and predict the same labels.

    The nearest-leaf rule pays the plan at every width. Gating it on d would keep the label identity only if the graph pass and predict moved together. Below d = 128 the hinted plan costs more than a scan by the plan's benchmark; this was not measured on the rows head.

    A plan without a hint stays open, with a bar to clear. A plan that takes the dispatch once per scan, or a gate on a statistic of the centres, could move the crossover. Any such proposal is measured on MiniLM and MPNet, the sets that lose here, as well as on MNIST.

    Alternatives rejected¶

    • Plan the centre rules at d ≥ 128 and k ≥ 256. MiniLM k = 256 1.91 → 2.77 G, MPNet 3.71 → 4.50 G: a regression on both embedding sets.
    • Plan them only at k ≥ 1 024. MiniLM still loses, 7.29 → 8.34 G.
    • Seed the threshold from every candidate's first stage when there is no hint. No coordinate is read twice and the answer does not change. Measured: MNIST k = 256 2.51 → 2.38 G, MiniLM 2.77 → 2.79 G, MPNet k = 1 024 12.58 → 12.31 G. It does not change which side of the scan the plan falls on.
    • Keep the scalar loop. It costs 3.5–8.1× the instructions of the full scan and gives the same labels on every set measured.
